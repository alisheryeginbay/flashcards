Q: "My friend *Alice Smith* likes jelly beans."
Why the highlighted noun phrase isn't set off by commas? Name the grammatical construction.
A: Because it's a restrictive appositive. I have many friends, but I'm restricting my statement to the one named Alice Smith.

Q: "Alice Smith, *my friend*, likes jelly beans."
Why the highlighted noun phrase is set off by commas? Name the grammatical construction.
A: Because it's a non-restrictive appositive. The fact that Alice Smith is my friend isn't necessary to identify her.

Q: "My brother, Nathan, is here."
"My brother Nathan is here."
What's the difference between two sentences?
A: The first sentence implies that the speaker has only one brother, and, as an aside, his name is Nathan.
The second sentence implies that he has several, and the one named Nathan is here.
I.e., first sentence is non-restrictive, second sentence is restrictive.

"The kit includes" is not a complete independent clause.

Q: What tests are commonly used to check if sequentially written adjectives require a comma?

Q: *It has now a dedicated event* or *It now has a dedicated event*? When does *now* go before or after "has"?
A: *It **now has** a dedicated event.*
- "has" + **thing** (possession) → *now* goes **before**: *It now has a garden.*
- "has" + **verb** (helper) → *now* goes **after**: *It has now finished.*
