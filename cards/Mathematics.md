Prove the existence.

Q: Take $(x+y)^3$. Prove the binomial theorem by expanding the example.
A: We have terms $xxx, xxy, xyx, \ldots, yyy$, or simply $x^3, x^2y, xy^2, y^3$.
If we consider these terms as an ordered selections, we calculate how many ways are there to choose $x$'s in 3 slots for each term that has a factor $x^k$ with $0 \leq k \leq 3$.
To get 3 $x$'s we have $\binom{3}{3}$, that is, $xxx$.
To get 2 $x$'s we have $\binom{3}{2}$, that is, $xxy = xyx = yxx$.
To get 1 $x$ we have $\binom{3}{1}$, that is, $xyy = yxx = yxy$.
To get 0 $x$ we have $\binom{3}{0}$, that is, $yyy$.
